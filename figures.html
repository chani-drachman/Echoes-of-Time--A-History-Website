Key Historical Figures & Relics of the Era

Three Prominent Figures

Shaul Avigur (1899–1978)

Shaul Avigur was the chief mastermind behind the entire clandestine immigration enterprise. Serving as the commander of Mossad LeAliyah Bet from its inception in 1939 until the establishment of Israel, Avigur directed a massive, underground international network from his headquarters in Paris and Tel Aviv. Operating with immense secrecy, he coordinated the purchase of ships, recruited foreign crews and sailors, navigated complex diplomatic networks across post-war Europe, and organized the secret routes through which tens of thousands of refugees escaped. Avigur’s quiet leadership, operational discipline, and vision transformed what began as local rescue missions into an organized fleet capable of challenging a major world power.

Yitzhak Sadeh (1890–1952)

Yitzhak Sadeh was a legendary military commander and the founder of the Palmach (the elite striking force of the Haganah). Recognizing that maritime operations required specialized combat units, Sadeh directed the Palmach to establish the Palyam (Palmach Naval Brigade) in 1945. Under his guidance, Palyam commanders were trained in seamanship, marine sabotage, and troop transportation. Palyam escorts boarded the Ha'apala vessels in European ports, took command during the voyages, provided physical protection to the refugees, and led resistance efforts when British forces boarded the ships. Sadeh’s leadership bridged military strategy with humanitarian rescue, creating the foundation for the future Israeli Navy.

Yossi Harel (1918–2008)

Yossi Harel was one of the most famous Palyam ship commanders, best known for commanding four major immigration vessels, including the iconic SS Exodus 1947 (originally named President Warfield). In July 1947, Harel commanded the Exodus, which carried 4,515 Holocaust survivors from France to Palestine. When surrounded and rammed by British warships off the coast of Haifa, Harel made the tactical decision to surrender after a bloody clash on board to save refugee lives. The British deported the passengers back to Europe on prison ships, a spectacle covered extensively by international media. Harel’s calm leadership during the crisis turned the Exodus into an international symbol of moral resistance, drastically undermining British authority in Palestine.
